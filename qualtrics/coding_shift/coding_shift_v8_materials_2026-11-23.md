# Part A. Design

## A1. Factors

Two factors, between subjects, four cells. Both are carried by the supervisor's written reply to the first note.

| Factor | Levels |
|---|---|
| Politeness of the rejection | High, low |
| Constructiveness of the rejection | High, low |

## A2. Cover story

The participant is hired for one shift as a Feedback Coder. The job is to sort comments into five categories. The coding scheme is under review this week. The coding supervisor decides on the rules. A note to the supervisor is requested once, after batch 1. After that, sending anything is optional. The supervisor rates each coder at the end of the shift, and the rating sets a bonus.

## A4. Measures

| Construct | Measure |
|---|---|
| Voice frequency | Number of batch 3 comments reported to the supervisor, 0 to 8. Whether a last note was written. Six-item scale. |
| Voice quality improvement effort | Coded content of what was sent: a count, an answer to the supervisor's concern, a workable fix. Ticks and time on the last note page. Four-item scale. Non-voicers score 0. |
| Issue raised | The repeated two-problem issue, the new no-category issue, or both |
| Task attribution | Five items |
| Social attribution | Three items |
| Manipulation checks | Politeness, eight items. Constructiveness, six items. |

All rating scales use five points: 1 strongly disagree, 2 disagree, 3 neither agree nor disagree, 4 agree, 5 strongly agree.

### The supervisor's reply · as now

The reply is written for each participant's own note, under fixed rules for each cell. It has two paragraphs. The first has 18 to 26 words and the second 42 to 52. The second paragraph had 36 to 46 words until 26 November 2026; the user raised it by six words in all four cells so that the come-back line fits without cramping the constructive replies. The four backup texts below were written for the earlier limit and are a few words shorter than a generated reply. Every reply turns the suggestion down. The four texts below show the style of each cell. They are the ones used if the service fails.

**High politeness, high constructiveness**

> Hi there, thanks for raising this; I can see the care behind it. I'm afraid I can't make this change as it stands.
>
> Each comment is counted once, and coders are checked for the same label; this change would unsettle both. If you could bring it back with solid numbers on how many comments were affected, and that were a large share, I'd gladly review the rule.

**High politeness, low constructiveness**

> Hi there, thank you for writing this up; I can see the care you took. I'm afraid I can't make this change.
>
> A change like this reaches well past one batch, and it touches how the whole project is set up; other things weigh on it too. If something changes, I'd be glad to look at this again. Thanks again for taking the time to raise it.

**Low politeness, high constructiveness**

> Got your note. I'm not making this change to the coding. The case for it doesn't hold up.
>
> Each comment is counted once, and coders are checked on giving the same label; this change would break both. Bring it back with solid numbers on how many comments were affected. A large share gets the rule reviewed. That's all I have on it.

**Low politeness, low constructiveness**

> Got your note. I'm not making this change to the coding. The idea doesn't hold up for this project.
>
> A change like this reaches well past one batch, and it touches how the whole project is set up and run; other things weigh on it too. Bring it back if something actually changes on the project side. That's all I have on it.

Low politeness is aimed at the proposal and at the supervisor's time. It never attacks the person.

Since 26 November 2026 (user request) the two high-constructiveness cells tell the coder to bring the suggestion back with solid numbers. Before that they only said that the decision needs numbers, because the coder had no way to answer; in version 8 the coder can report comments in batch 3 and leave a note at the end. The first wording of that day used the verb "report". The user then asked for another word, because the batch 3 button reads "Report this comment to the coding supervisor" and the reply would prime it. No reply uses "report" in any form now. All four cells share the verb, "bring it back", and differ in the terms: solid numbers under high constructiveness, "if something changes" under low. The service checks both rules and asks for one rewrite when a reply breaks one.
