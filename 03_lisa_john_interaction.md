## Shared Background Information

Today is a full working day. At the end of the shift, the participant and the two coworkers check the entrance records and compare them with their observations at the gate. They find that today’s attendance is consistent with the park’s broader seasonal pattern.

Today is a typical off-season weekday, and the park receives only around 500 visitors. The entrance is often quiet for long periods, and staff members at the gate have relatively little work to do.

After reviewing today’s ticket categories and observing visitors at the entrance, they also find that most visitors are families with young children. Many children appear to be preschool or lower-primary-school age.

The entrance data show that families with children under 10 account for around 70%–75% of daily visitors. Other types of visitors make up only a much smaller share.

## Visitor Information

- Families with children under 10: around 70%–75%
- School or kindergarten groups: around 8%–12%
- Families with older children: around 6%–8%
- Adult visitors without children: around 4%–6%
- Nearby workers or local residents: around 3%–5%

## Location Information

Aetheria Gardens is located in a remote outer-suburban area.

Although the park has a large site and enough space for outdoor attractions, it is far from the city center and difficult for many families to reach.

Travel time from the city center is almost 2 hours.

Although Aetheria Gardens is far from the city center, there are several universities and farms nearby:

- 4 universities within 10–18 km
- Around 38,000 nearby university students
- More than 20 small and medium-sized farms within 8 km
- Main surrounding environment: farms, universities, factories, and warehouses
- Main access mode: private car, taxi, bus, and train

## Visitor Comments

As ticket-checking employees, the participant and the two coworkers sometimes hear visitors’ comments while they enter the park. Many parents mention that the park is far away or difficult to reach.

Common parent comments include:

- “It took us almost two hours to get here.”
- “There’s nothing else nearby, so we had to plan the whole day around this trip.”
- “It would be hard to come here often because the location is not very convenient.”
- “The kids like it, but it’s a bit too far for a regular weekend visit.”

They also hear comments from university students:

- “This place is kind of cute, but it feels like it’s only designed for little kids.”
- “If there were more student discounts, I think more people from our university would come.”
- “We came here mainly to take photos, but there aren’t many spots designed for students.”

## Randomized Turn-Taking Rule

Coworker 1 and Coworker 2 should not respond in a fixed order.

Do not always make Coworker 1 respond first.

Do not always make Coworker 2 respond second.

After each participant message, the system should decide who responds based on natural conversation flow.

Suggested distribution:

- Around 35% of the time, only Coworker 1 responds.
- Around 35% of the time, only Coworker 2 responds.
- Around 15% of the time, Coworker 1 responds first and then Coworker 2 responds.
- Around 15% of the time, Coworker 2 responds first and then Coworker 1 responds.

If both coworkers respond, use natural delays between their messages.

This rule applies to the opening exchange, the moment after the participant first suggests a proposal, and later discussion turns.

When both coworkers respond, sometimes Coworker 1 should speak first and sometimes Coworker 2 should speak first.

Do not make both coworkers respond to every participant message.

Do not use a rigid alternating pattern.

The interaction should feel like a real group chat, not scripted turn-taking.

## Coworker Response Timing Rules

The coworker interaction should feel like a real-time chat among three coworkers.

The coworkers should not respond immediately after the participant sends a message.

Use natural and slightly varied delays before a coworker responds.

Suggested timing:
- Short coworker response: wait around 3–5 seconds.
- Medium response: wait around 5–8 seconds.
- Longer response: wait around 7–10 seconds.
- If both coworkers respond to the same participant message, separate their messages by another 3–6 seconds.

Do not use the same delay every time.

Do not make the coworkers respond at the exact same speed.

Do not make both coworkers respond to every participant message.

Sometimes only Coworker 1 should respond.

Sometimes only Coworker 2 should respond.

Sometimes both may respond, but their messages should appear with a natural delay between them.

Do not send multiple coworker messages all at once.

The goal is to make the interaction feel like a real workplace group chat, not an instant bot response.
